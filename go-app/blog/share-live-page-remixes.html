A screenshot shows one moment. A shared remix link shows the idea itself, ready to run. Here is how to share web page experiments so teammates and friends can open, read and build on them.

Why links beat screenshots

When you send a link, the other person sees the same live page and can read the JavaScript and CSS behind it. They can change a value, press Run and see the result. That makes feedback concrete: "try 18px here" becomes a quick edit instead of a long message.

Create a remix worth sharing

  1. Open WebFiddle and enter a start URL.
  2. Edit the CSS and JavaScript until the page looks the way you imagined.
  3. Press Save (Ctrl+S) to store your fiddle.
  4. Press Share to get the link.

Write a helpful title and description

Titles such as "Larger checkout button" explain themselves. Add a description that says what changed and what you would like people to look at. A good description saves everyone time and makes your link easy to find again.

Organize the code for readers

  • Group related CSS rules together and keep selectors short.
  • Start the JavaScript with a one line summary of what it does.
  • Prefer small functions with clear names.
  • Keep one idea per fiddle, and save a new fiddle for the next idea.

Use remixes for different jobs

  • Design review. Show a color or spacing change on the real page.
  • Bug reports. Share a fiddle that reproduces how a page behaves with a particular style.
  • Teaching. Give students a starting fiddle and ask them to modify it.
  • Personal tweaks. Keep a fiddle that tidies a site you use every day.

Ask for specific feedback

Close your message with a question: "Does the new layout feel easier to scan?" Specific questions bring specific answers, and the link lets reviewers try their own variations right away.

More places to explore

Build a simulated site in SitesSim, talk through an idea with ChatGibidy, or make 3D models with SimplexGen, then bring the results into a WebFiddle remix.

Build on other people's ideas

Every shared fiddle is a starting point. When a colleague sends a remix, open it, change a rule and save your own version. Your link then shows your take, and the original stays as it was. This friendly back and forth moves a design from a rough sketch to a polished choice with a few quick rounds.

A simple review checklist

  • Does the page read well at phone width and desktop width?
  • Is the text easy to read against its background?
  • Do buttons and links have comfortable sizes for touch?
  • Does the JavaScript run cleanly after the page loads?

Share the checklist with your link, and reviewers can respond point by point.

Keep your fiddles tidy

Retitle fiddles when ideas evolve, and describe the final state in the description. A tidy collection of remixes becomes a handy portfolio of experiments that you and your team can revisit whenever a similar question comes up.
